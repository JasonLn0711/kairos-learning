# CEH M01-07 | Reference answer

The company has confused three different questions: whether a defined management-system scope has been assessed, whether this alert is a false positive, and whether the incident has been resolved.

An assessment alone does not prove that this download was authorized. Closing an alert changes its notification or tracking status; it does not prove that the underlying issue is gone.

Start with two kinds of evidence:

1. Activity evidence: the account, timestamps, downloaded resources, access results, and completeness of the relevant records. Establish what happened, not just what the alert title says.
2. Authorization and business context: what the account was allowed to do, whether an approved backup or work activity explains the downloads, and the actual scope of the assessment and system.

If there are signs of unauthorized activity, evaluate and respond through the approved process. If the activity was legitimate, document the evidence for that conclusion. If evidence is insufficient, keep the conclusion open rather than declaring either perfect safety or a confirmed compromise.

Risk management continues: check that controls work, responsibilities are clear, and circumstances have not changed.
