# M02-07 Reference Answers

## answer-source-independence | Check 1: Shared source

Answer B: One original source, three publication locations. Preserve the locations for traceability, and use an independently maintained current official record to verify identity and present role. A counts republication as independent confirmation; C loses the date context; D substitutes article count for source and time verification.

## answer-deep-web | Check 2: Private mailbox

Answer B: Deep-web content. A private authenticated mailbox is generally outside ordinary search indexes and serves content under authentication and permission rules. Surface-web content is publicly indexable. Tor onion services use a particular network and .onion addresses. A data breach is an exposure event, evaluated separately from index classification.

## answer-exposure-claim | Check 3: Verify an exposure claim

Preserve details such as the publisher or source location, publication time and time zone, claimed data year and categories, and the service's unverified status. Under authorization, compare owned account or system logs and formal vendor incident announcements. An example record is: “On November 1, the service relayed an unverified exposure claim concerning data allegedly from 2022; incident response is checking relevant records.” Keep minimum necessary information and mask personal data and secrets.

## answer-defensive-action | Check 4: Content and process protection

Content adjustment: Have the owner revise the photograph to remove unnecessary internal project details, then verify the revision from a public-visitor view. Process safeguard: Publish the verified system entry point and confirm payment, login, or account-change requests through known contacts and the approval workflow. This reduces details available for pretexting and supplies a traceable verification path for requests.
