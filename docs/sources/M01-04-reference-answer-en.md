# M01-04 Quick-check reference answer (collapsed by default on the website; not a learner response)

The attack surface includes the company's interfaces for receiving external email, along with the people and processes involved in receiving email and signing in. This does not mean the inbox “has a vulnerability.” Rather, these are points where external messages can reach the company's people and systems, so they need appropriate protection.

The possible attack vector is phishing: impersonating an IT notification, directing the employee to a fake website to surrender credentials, and then trying to impersonate the account holder. However, the only observed event here is that the phishing email arrived. The later steps have not been established.

Therefore, we cannot yet conclude that the account has been compromised. Further evidence is needed about whether the employee clicked, entered credentials, whether authentication and login succeeded, and whether any improper access occurred. Even if the employee entered a password, that does not mean the attacker necessarily logged in successfully; other authentication and access restrictions may still apply.

Along this possible path, review email protection, verification of notifications through an independent channel, phishing-resistant authentication and sign-in records. Reading this reference answer still does not mean that the learner has answered or demonstrated understanding.
